# Counting Kings — A Data History of King's College Cambridge

**Live presentation:** [nfitzhenry.github.io/counting-kings](https://nfitzhenry.github.io/counting-kings/)

An interactive data history of 170 years of admissions to King's College Cambridge (1797–1970), built from the College's printed registers of admissions.

## About the project

This project digitises and analyses the biographical registers of King's College Cambridge, creating a prosopographical database of over 2,500 members. The presentation explores:

- **Social composition and access** — how the social background of King's members changed over time, from an exclusively Eton-fed fellowship college before 1861 to a broader national intake thereafter
- **Educational pipelines** — which schools fed King's, and how that pipeline evolved across 170 years
- **Career trajectories** — the ecclesiastical, academic, legal, medical, military, and colonial careers members pursued
- **Military service and mortality** — participation and deaths across conflicts from the Napoleonic Wars to the Second World War

## Data

The dataset is built from five overlapping printed registers of admissions. The current version covers the Withers register (1797–1925): 2,576 biographical entries, ~125 fields per entry, extracted using a combination of regex pipelines and local LLM processing.

## Research context

Part of a broader programme of research by Nicholas Fitzhenry at [CAMPOP](https://www.campop.geog.cam.ac.uk/) (Cambridge Group for the History of Population and Social Structure), combining newly digitised archival sources with quantitative demographic methods.
